# Scissor Pick policy recovery — 3 November 2026

## Current measured state

The existing Scissor Pick data contains 70 train, 20 validation, and 10 test episodes, balanced over 10 grid cells. Every episode has exactly one expert OPEN-to-CLOSE transition. At the CLOSE frame, the recorded fingers are still open and begin moving on the next frame, which is the expected observation/action timing. This audit found no evidence of a reversed gripper label or one-frame shift.

The V3 Scissor gripper pilot failed its offline gate: validation CLOSE-onset recall 55%, premature CLOSE 26.25%, and hold recall 93.33%. The earlier V2 onset recall was 5%. Both V2 and V3 failed the paired closed-loop Pick rollouts. Do not spend another full training run on the same representation.

A *training* trajectory transformed with the validation object's simulator ground-truth pose lifted Scissor in **5 of 5** distinct validation starts. The evaluator chooses the nearest of 70 training templates; validation expert actions are never replayed. See `datasets/DP_evaluation/scissor_template_oracle_pilot5_20261103/summary.json`. This is an oracle-assisted upper-bound diagnostic, not a learned-policy or camera-autonomous success rate.

The current camera data include RGB, depth, calibration, and ground-truth instance masks for offline diagnosis. A naive 3D centroid of the *ground-truth* target mask is still about 3–4 cm from the expert Scissor grasp XY label across the static cameras on validation. See `datasets/DP_evaluation/scissor_target_geometry_audit_20261103.json`. Scissor's physical mask center is therefore not a safe grasp coordinate; a shape-aware pose or grasp estimator is required.

A first train-only linear correction of **front-camera RF-DETR B mask + depth** was evaluated on all 20 held-out validation episodes: Scissor was detected in 20/20, grasp XY error median **0.94 cm**, 90th percentile **2.08 cm**, 12/20 within 1 cm and 17/20 within 2 cm. See `datasets/DP_evaluation/scissor_detector_grasp_audit_20261103.json`. These are offline pose errors, not grasp outcomes. The correction was fit using training ground-truth masks and grasp labels; validation inputs used predicted RF-DETR masks. A naive point-cloud principal axis had about 35° median orientation error modulo 180° on validation, so yaw remains unresolved.

**Implemented after that audit:** a Scissor-specific 2D depth-cloud shape-registration estimator replaced the naive principal axis. Its reference shape comes from training masks and train object poses. The query mask comes from RF-DETR B, and the query depth/calibration come from a newly captured front-camera frame. This pose drives nearest-training-template selection and action transformation. The first frozen recorded-frame test was 8/10; the later live-camera repeat on those same starts was 10/10. See `datasets/DP_evaluation/scissor_live_camera_test10_20261103/RESULTS.md`. This is an engineered retrieval controller, not a retrained Diffusion Policy, and the repeated test is not a fresh blind benchmark.

## Next implementation, in order

1. **Perception gate.** The implemented shape-registration estimator now supplies Scissor XY and yaw from RF-DETR-Seg B plus live depth/calibration; keep its output and failure flags visible. Improve occlusion handling and verify against new, independently recorded scenes. The prior linear XY correction remains an audit baseline, not the deployed estimator.
2. **Action gate.** Train a Scissor-only target-relative policy: observations include measured robot state and the estimated grasp pose; actions are local TCP motion relative to that pose, with a separate CLOSE/OPEN event prediction and explicit post-CLOSE lift examples. Score onset, premature close, grasp distance, and lift in closed loop. The 5/5 oracle-template result is the positive control for this representation, not the final controller.
3. **Recovery data.** If the target-relative policy is correct on expert frames but fails closed loop, record expert corrections from states reached by the policy, including lateral misses, hovering before CLOSE, premature CLOSE, and post-CLOSE failure to lift. Add these only to train; keep held-out seeds/cells/yaws fixed.
4. **More scenes only after gates.** If the camera grasp estimator or target-relative policy fails its small pilot, collect several hundred additional Scissor-only scenes with varied cells, yaw, lighting and nearby distractors. Preserve raw RGB-D, instance masks, camera calibration, robot state, expert grasp pose, stage, and action at the same timestamp. Use a scene-level split; no frames from one episode cross splits.
5. **Acceptance test.** Freeze all thresholds and weights, then run at least 50 newly recorded Scissor Pick starts without simulator target pose or validation/test expert actions. For the retrieval controller, training templates are part of the method and must be disclosed. Require at least 30 physical lift successes out of 50 for the requested 60% point estimate. Report the confidence interval and failures by cell/yaw/lighting. This is Pick only; Place needs its own gate.

The evidence rules out a simple reversed-gripper-label fix. It does not prove that the dataset alone or network alone is the sole cause. The present model lacks a reliable selected-instance grasp pose and corrective training states, so re-recording the same demonstration distribution and only increasing epochs is not a justified next step.
